# 00. Research notes: what popular cards and children's books look like

Owner: Head of Design. Read and written 2026-10-11 (all "date read" values below are that day unless shown). Audience: owner, CMO, CTO, CX, Greet Guide authors.

## How to read this

Tags: **[V]** I fetched the page and read its (tool-summarised) text, so exact wording should be re-checked before it goes into an ad; **[S]** secondary: a search-result snippet, a vendor or agency blog, or coverage of another source; **[U]** unverified, or my own judgement. Vendor and printer blogs are marketing, so they are directional only. "Not found" means I looked and could not find it, not that it does not exist.

Rules followed: styles are described by medium, technique, line, palette, composition, mood and era. No living artist, studio or franchise is used as a style reference anywhere in the library. Book titles and illustrators appear below only as sales or award facts, and no style string refers to them. Web content was treated as data, not instructions.

Limits of this pass: no access to Circana BookScan, Amazon's live bestseller pages, Etsy search data, Pinterest's own report, Hallmark or Papier sales figures, or Meta/Pinterest ad libraries. Popularity below is therefore ordinal (high / medium / niche / emerging), built from several weak signals, never a measured share.

## 2. What is actually popular

### Children's books (sales and awards)

- **Sales, 2025 US units [V, R01].** The top picture and board-book entries are classics and character brands: *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* 565,688 (backlist); *Brown Bear, Brown Bear* 516,043; *Goodnight Moon* 363,673; *The Rainbow Fish* 357,861; frontlist *100 First Words* 348,361, *Potty Time with Bean* 343,781, *Little Blue Truck and Racer Red* 224,547, *You're My Little Sweet Pea* 121,426; *Dragons Love Tacos* 192,670; *The Day the Crayons Quit* 140,812. The article gives no illustration styles. My attribution [U, R19]: tactile cut-paper collage, vintage gouache, soft painterly storybook, crayon and ink doodle, and clean digital cartoon are all in the top 70. Takeaway [S]: no single look dominates. Warm, hand-made-looking, high-contrast, simple-shape art sells for babies and toddlers, and playful cartoon sells for 4-7.
- **Awards [V, R03].** 2026 Caldecott Medal: *Fireworks* (illus. Cátia Chien). Honors: *Every Monday Mabel*, *Our Lake*, *Stalactite & Stalagmite*, *Sundust*. Technique is not stated in any source I could open ("not found"); R02 says the medalist deliberately mixed unfamiliar media including scratchboard and chose them by palette. Award lists skew painterly, mixed-media and graphic: committees reward distinct hand-made craft. That is evidence that texture is valued, not evidence of what families buy for a custom gift.
- **Trend reports [V/S].** R04: hand-drawn and imperfect rising because AI imagery looks alike; comfort, nostalgia and hope. R05: watercolor still the "nostalgic" default for ages 3-6; minimalist for 0-3; collage and mixed media for parents who like art-house; tactile digital rising; hyper-expressive characters, neon-pastel palettes and 3D for action are 2026 trends. R04 also lists big-eyed, emotion-led character design as a major influence.
- **Competitors [V/S].** ToonyStory offers 15 styles: 3D animated, 2D cartoon, modern 3D, soft realism, soft and dreamy, watercolor, storybook classic, anime, realistic, comic, pencil sketch, plastic brick, paper craft, voxel pixel, playful cartoon [V, R09]. Magic Story sells a "Pixar-quality" cinematic look [V, R10]. Smaller tools list watercolor, cartoon, 3D cartoon, clay animation, sketch, felt craft, comic [S, R12]. The category leaders (Wonderbly, Hooray Heroes) ship one fixed house look per title [R18]. The AI-book market is split: leaders sell hand-drawn trust, AI entrants sell a menu of 8-15 looks, dominated by 3D-animated, cartoon and watercolor.
- **Parent attitude [V, R20].** Human-edited work is accepted; labelled AI art is preferred. Visible hand-made texture (paper grain, brush marks) signals craft [S, R04, R15].

### Greeting cards

- **What sells [S].** Humor first (Moonpig's top 2026 US birthday card is a funny pop-up, R13; Amazon's new-release lists lean funny, foil and pop-up, R14). Illustrated sentiment is strongest on Etsy: watercolor and punny cartoon animals (R14). Premium stationery is letterpress plus gold foil (R17, R14).
- **Trends [V, R06-R08].** Handwritten script and hand-drawn art (R06); bold geometrics and earthy palettes (R06); bold, colorful and maximalist for congratulations and holidays (R07, R08); minimalist and fine-line for weddings and sympathy (R07); soft pastels with metallic accents for new baby and Valentine (R07); watercolor for Mother's Day (R07). In holiday cards, illustration is an accent around type or photos, not full-bleed art (R08). The pen-pal and handwritten-letter revival on Pinterest (R16) supports hand-lettered type and stationery looks.
- **Counter-signal [S].** A UK card-shop blog (shop.pogofandango.co.uk, seen in search results, not opened) argues that AI-generated and mass-produced imagery is falling out of favor and that visible hand-drawn lines win. Design implication: card looks need visible human-feeling texture and a strong type plan.
- **Not found.** Papier's best-selling designs or style mix; Hallmark's sales by style; any ranked share of watercolor vs cartoon vs foil on Etsy; Pinterest's own 2026 report text; Adobe Creative Trends 2026 full text.

## 4. Trend notes that shape the library

1. **Hand-made texture is the 2026 counter-position to AI sameness** (R04, R15, R20). Every style string asks for a visible physical medium (grain, brush, paper), and the library leads with those looks.
2. **Humor and bold color sell cards** (R08, R13, R14), so bold pop, flat graphic and scratchy-ink humor are first-class card styles, not kids-only.
3. **Type is part of the card style** (R06, R07, R16): script and mixed fonts for sentiment, oversized sans for bold. The library carries a type plan per card style, and type is added by us, never drawn by the model.
4. **Foil cannot be drawn by an image model.** Printers apply it as a spot finish. Foil looks are offered as a printed gold-look illustration only, with the caveat shown to the customer.
5. **The AI-book menu is dominated by "movie" looks** (R09, R10). Customers will ask for them by franchise name. The library offers generic equivalents (soft 3D animated, anime-inspired, cinematic) and the IP layer maps the phrase to a style id, never into a prompt.
6. **Consistency is the real constraint for books, not taste.** Flat, outlined, limited-palette styles hold characters best; loose watercolor, collage and photoreal hold worst. See the render findings in 01 and each style file.
