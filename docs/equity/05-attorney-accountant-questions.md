# Questions for the owner's attorney and accountant

We give research and modelling, not legal or tax advice.

## Attorney
1. Entity: LLC or C-corp (see `docs/legal/04-business-setup.md`)? How does a dynamic ledger fit an LLC operating agreement versus a corporation's stock issuance (Moyer-style agreements use a pre-formation or operating agreement; which does your state favour)?
2. Is a "slices" ledger enforceable as a contract before conversion? What must the signed policy say?
3. Vesting: slices are effectively earned as they go. Is any additional vesting, cliff or repurchase right needed? Does the dynamic model create any "grant" at each entry?
4. Leaver definitions: draft "good reason", "for cause", resignation. Is reducing a leaver's cash to 1x, retrieving non-cash slices, or requiring cash repayment without interest enforceable in our state? Which of Moyer's four quadrants should we adopt?
5. Trigger events: how do slices convert to shares or units at a financing, profit distribution or sale? Formula, timing, who calculates, who audits.
6. Idea slices: are awards for ideas (our departure from the base method) acceptable, and how do we avoid IP-ownership disputes? Do all partners need an IP assignment so ideas and work belong to the company?
7. Securities law: does issuing equity for services to a small number of partners need any filing or exemption?
8. Partners who are minors, non-US residents or have other employers (moonlighting and IP conflicts)?
9. Sweat equity versus employment: does tracking hours create wage-and-hour or employee-classification risk?
10. Records: retention, who holds the ledger, dispute process, and whether approval by one other partner is sufficient.

## Accountant
1. Tax at grant: does each approved entry create taxable income for a partner (profits interest vs stock for services, 83(b) election timing in a corporation)? How do slices affect an LLC's partnership capital accounts?
2. Valuation: how should we document fair-market base rates so they withstand scrutiny?
3. Cash and expense contributions: how to treat unreimbursed expenses (capital contribution vs loan), and the receipt standard.
4. Capital contributions versus loans: if a partner's cash is later repaid, how is it reversed in the ledger and the books?
5. Bookkeeping: how to record non-cash contributions (time, equipment use) in the books, if at all.
6. State and payroll implications if partners later move from slices to pay.
