# Christmas cards with your words and your picture

Make a Christmas card that sounds like your family and not like a stack of the same ten designs. Tell us who it's for and what you'd say, and we draw the front for them.

## How it works

1. **Tell us.** Chat with the Greet Guide or fill in a short form: who it's for, what you want to say, how it should feel.
2. **Check the look.** You see a preview before anything is final. Change what's off.
3. **Approve and print.** When you say yes, we print the 5 by 7 folded card on real paper.

## What to write in a Christmas card

Most people stall on the inside. A few ways in, depending on who's reading:

- **Start with one true thing from this year.** "Thank you for driving Dad to every appointment" lands harder than "Wishing you a joyful season."
- **Keep it to three or four lines.** A short note people read twice beats a long one they skim.
- **Name them.** Use the first name of the person, not "Dear friend."
- **End with something forward-looking.** "Come and see us in the new house" gives them a reason to write back.

If you'd rather not start from a blank page, the Greet Guide asks one question at a time and helps you shape the lines. It's your card, and nothing is made until you say yes.

## Ideas by who it's for

**Grandparents.** Keep the message plain and warm, and mention something small they'd recognise, such as the way they always burn the first batch of cookies. Large, easy-to-read wording works well here.

**Friends far away.** Say what you miss. A card that mentions the thing you did together last winter will mean more than a generic greeting.

**Neighbours, teachers, the people who helped this year.** Short and specific: what they did, and that it mattered.

**Your partner or your kids.** A funny card can be as loved as a sentimental one. Pick the tone you'd actually use at the kitchen table.

**Whole-family cards.** Describe the household in a sentence or two and we can draw it as characters. We draw invented, illustrated people, never lookalikes of anyone famous or any cartoon character.

## How GreetHub makes it personal

The picture on the front is drawn for this card: your recipient, your tone, your scene. You choose the feeling (warm, funny, cozy) and an illustration style, and you see the result before you commit. The words inside are yours. We don't add stock verse.

You can have the card mailed to you to sign and send, or mailed straight to one recipient. Mailing one card to a long list of people in one order isn't something we offer yet.

## Template that fits

- **merry-and-bright, "Merry and Bright"** (card, heartfelt, cozy gouache style): a snowy cabin with glowing windows and a lit fir tree under a night sky. A good start for a quiet, warm message you then make your own.

Other templates, such as "Just Wanted You to Know" (just-wanted-you-to-know), can carry a Christmas message too if the daisies don't feel off-season to you. Start from any template and change the occasion in the chat.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I write my own message?**
Yes. You can write every word, or let the Greet Guide help you draft and then edit it.

**Will the card look like my family?**
If you describe your family, we draw invented characters from that description. They're illustrations, not photographs, and we don't copy known characters.

**Can I see it before I pay?**
You can make one watermarked preview as a guest, with no account. A free account lets you keep it and see a full-size proof before you order.

**Is a Christmas card a good gift for someone who has everything?**
A card with a real, specific note inside is often what people keep. If you want something bigger, a [personalized picture book](/occasions/christmas-picture-books) is the longer version of the same idea.

**When will it arrive?**
Delivery timing depends on the print shop and where it's going, and we don't promise a date here. Check the order-by notice in the creator and the proof screen when you order.
