# A Thanksgiving card that says what you mean

Thanksgiving is a good excuse to tell someone, in writing, what they did for you. Tell us who it's for, add a few lines of your own, and we draw the front for them.

## How it works

1. **Tell us.** Chat with the Greet Guide or fill in a short form: who it's for, what you want to thank them for, how it should feel.
2. **Check the look.** See a preview before anything is final, and change what's off.
3. **Approve and print.** When you say yes, we print the 5 by 7 folded card on real paper.

## What to write in a Thanksgiving card

Gratitude is easy to feel and oddly hard to put on paper. These help:

- **Name one thing.** "Thank you for holding the ladder while I fixed the gutter" beats "Thanks for everything."
- **Say what it did for you.** The effect is the point: "It meant I wasn't doing it alone."
- **Keep it small enough to finish.** Three or four sentences. A card people actually mail is better than a perfect one that stays in a drawer.
- **Skip the formula.** You don't need "grateful and blessed." Use your own words.

Short openers if you're stuck: "I don't say this enough, but..." / "This year, you were the person who..." / "Something I noticed that I want you to know..."

## Ideas by who it's for

**Family hosting the meal.** Thank them for the work nobody sees: the planning, the shopping, the dishes. A card to the cook is a lovely thing to leave on the counter.

**Friends who showed up this year.** Name the moment they helped. People rarely hear which small thing mattered.

**Teachers, coaches and carers.** A short, specific thank-you for the year. A funny card can work as well as a serious one, if that's how you talk to them.

**Colleagues and neighbours.** Keep it brief and kind. One line about what you appreciate is plenty.

**Someone going through a hard year.** Say plainly that you're glad they're in your life. You don't need to fix anything.

## How GreetHub makes it personal

The picture on the front is drawn for this card, in a style and mood you choose, such as warm and quiet or light and funny. The words inside are yours. We don't add stock verse or pre-written poems.

If you'd rather not start from a blank page, the Greet Guide asks one question at a time and helps you shape your note. Nothing is made until you say yes.

You can have the card mailed to you or to one recipient. Mailing one card to a long list isn't something we offer yet. We draw invented, illustrated characters only, never lookalikes of real people or existing characters.

## Templates that fit

We're adding a Thanksgiving card template. For now, the closest fits are:

- **just-wanted-you-to-know, "Just Wanted You to Know"** (card, heartfelt, ink wash): daisies tied with twine on a soft wash. Quiet and sincere, a good frame for a plain thank-you.
- **thanks-coach, "Thanks, Coach"** (card, funny, paper-cut collage): a whistle, clipboard and crooked trophy. Good if the thanks come with a laugh. Change the scene in the chat for a non-sports thank-you.

You can also start from a blank card and describe the scene, such as a long table with mismatched chairs or a pie cooling by a window.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this only for Thanksgiving?**
No. The same kind of card works as a thank-you any time of year. Pick the occasion in the creator.

**Can I write my own message?**
Yes. Write all of it, or ask the Greet Guide to help you draft it and then edit.

**Can I see it before I pay?**
You can make one watermarked preview as a guest, with no account. A free account lets you keep it and see a full-size proof before you order.

**Can it go straight to the person?**
Yes, mailed to one recipient. Sending to many recipients in one order isn't available yet.

**Will it arrive before the holiday?**
We don't promise dates on this page. Printing and postage take time, so look for the order-by notice in the creator, and consider a digital card if you're short on time.
