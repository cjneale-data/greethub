# Teacher thank you cards that say something real

Teachers read a lot of "Thank you for everything" cards. The ones they keep tell them what they actually did: the week a shy kid finally raised a hand, the book that changed what a child reads. A GreetHub card helps you write that, then puts it in an illustration made for them.

## How it works

1. **Tell us about the teacher and the year.** A name, the subject or grade, and one thing they did that mattered.
2. **Pick a tone.** Warm and sincere, or light and funny for a favorite coach or music teacher.
3. **Preview, then order.** You see the card before it prints and can edit anything.

## What to write in a teacher thank you card

A good note has three parts. It is easy to do in a few sentences.

1. **Thank them for something specific.** "Thank you for staying after class to explain fractions again."
2. **Say what changed.** "Now Jonah says he is good at math. He was not saying that in September."
3. **End with warmth.** "We are lucky he had you."

Some starting lines to borrow:

- "You made Monday mornings something she looked forward to."
- "He talks about your class at the dinner table."
- "Thank you for noticing what the rest of us had missed."
- "You made space for every kid in that room."

Aim for three to six sentences. Teachers do not need an essay. They need to know the work was seen.

## Ideas for different situations

- **End of the school year.** Look back on what the child learned and how they grew. This is the most common moment and the one most teachers get a pile of cards for. A specific line stands out.
- **From a whole class.** Collect one short sentence from each child, and send it as one card. Tell us how you would like the lines arranged.
- **A coach, tutor, or music teacher.** A funny card often fits well, particularly for sports or arts.
- **A bus driver, aide, or office staff.** The people who make school work are often forgotten. A card to them is welcome.
- **Mid-year or after a hard stretch.** A card in February can matter more than one in June, when the year's work is taken for granted.

A small note on tone: keep it about the teacher's work, not a gift. Many schools have their own guidelines on gifts, and a card needs no price tag.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a teacher thank you card be?**
Three to six sentences is plenty. One specific memory is better than a long general paragraph.

**Can the whole class sign one card?**
Yes. Collect a sentence from each student and add them to your brief. We will help arrange them.

**Should I include my child's name?**
You can. A teacher will know exactly who it is from. Leave out last names, and do not include anything you would not want on a card that might be posted on a classroom wall.

**Do I need to upload a photo?**
No. Photos are optional, and for a school card we would suggest skipping them.

**Is a card enough?**
Yes. Many teachers say a sincere note is the part they keep. If you want to add something, a small gesture within your school's guidelines is fine.
