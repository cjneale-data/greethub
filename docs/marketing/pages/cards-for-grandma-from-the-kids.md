# Cards for Grandma, from the kids

Grandma already has plenty of things. What she keeps are the words the kids say, the funny way they say them, and anything with their handwriting on it. A GreetHub card or picture book gathers those into one thing she can hold.

## How it works

1. **Share what the kids are like.** Their names, ages, what they call her, the things they say that make you laugh.
2. **Choose a card or a book.** A folded 5x7 card for a quick hello, or an 8.5x8.5 hardcover picture book of 24 or 32 pages for a story.
3. **Preview, then order.** You see it before it is printed and can change the words.

## What kids can say to Grandma

Young children rarely write paragraphs, and that is fine. Short and true works best. You can ask each child one question and write down the answer exactly as they say it:

- What is something Grandma always does at your house?
- What is your favorite thing to do with Grandma?
- If you could give Grandma anything, what would it be?
- What does Grandma's kitchen smell like?

Keep their phrasing, even when it is not quite correct. "Grandma makes the good pancakes, the ones that are not burned" is worth more than a polished line. For older kids and teens, a single honest sentence is plenty: a memory, a thank you, a promise to visit.

## Ideas that grandparents keep

- **A story starring the kids.** A picture book where the kids go on a small adventure, with Grandma at the center of it. Think a rowboat at dusk, a garden, a rainy afternoon of baking.
- **The things she calls them.** Nicknames, songs, sayings. Put them on the page.
- **A card from a far-away family.** If you live a long way apart, tell us about the distance. A card can say what a phone call does not have time for.
- **A line from every grandchild.** Cousins can each add a sentence, so Grandma gets one card with everyone in it.
- **A birthday, a holiday, or no reason.** Grandparents' Day, a milestone birthday, or a Tuesday.

## How GreetHub makes it personal

You write the brief, and GreetHub turns it into original illustrations and words in the tone you pick, from tender to silly. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can the card come from several kids?**
Yes. List each child and what you want them to say. You can give each one a line, or write one shared message.

**Can we include a child's drawing?**
Not as a feature today. You can describe the drawing in your brief, and we can work its details into the illustration.

**Does Grandma need to be on the site?**
No. You make it, and she gets a printed card or book. She does not need an account.

**What if my kids are too young to say anything?**
Write it as a story about them. "Mia is two and says 'Gram-gram' like it is one word" is a good opening line.

**Is a book or a card better?**
A card is quick and easy to send. A book tells a story and can sit on a shelf. If you are unsure, start with a card.
