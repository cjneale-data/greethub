# What to write in a sympathy card

When someone is grieving, it is hard to find words, and harder still to feel that yours are enough. There is no perfect sentence. What matters is that you reached out, and that you meant it. Here is some help with the words.

## How it works

If you would like to write a card with GreetHub, it goes like this.

1. **Write what you would say in person.** Plain words are fine.
2. **Choose a quiet illustration,** such as flowers, a candle, or a landscape. We do not depict the person who has died.
3. **Read it over and decide.** You see the card before anything is printed, and you can change it or leave it.

## What to write

You do not need many words. A few sincere lines are enough.

- **Say that you are sorry.** "I am so sorry for the loss of your mother."
- **Use their name.** "I have been thinking about Daniel, and about all of you."
- **Share a small memory, if you have one.** "I will always remember how he laughed at his own jokes before he reached the end of them."
- **Offer something specific, and only if you mean it.** "I will call on Sunday. You do not need to answer." Avoid "let me know if you need anything." It leaves the work with the person grieving.
- **Close simply.** "With love," "Thinking of you," or "With deepest sympathy."

A short example you can adapt:

> *Dear Anna, I was so sorry to hear about your father. He was kind to me, and I will remember that. I am thinking of you and your family. With love, Priya*

## What to avoid

Some phrases are well meant but can hurt. People who are grieving often mention these:

- "He is in a better place" or "everything happens for a reason," unless you know their beliefs.
- "I know how you feel." Every loss is different.
- "At least..." in any form.
- Advice about how long to grieve or what to do next.
- Anything about yourself that pulls the focus from them.

If you do not share their faith, you can still be kind without pretending. "I am holding you in my thoughts" is respectful to nearly everyone.

## When it is hard to say anything

- **Late is fine.** A card that arrives weeks or months after the loss is still welcome. Grief does not end after the funeral, and the cards stop coming before it does.
- **Name the difficulty.** "I do not have the right words, but I wanted you to know I care."
- **Write about the person, not the death.** A memory, a quality, a thing they taught you.
- **Pet loss counts.** If an animal was family, say so. It was a loss.
- **Colleagues and neighbors.** Keep it brief, kind, and free of detail you would not say at their door.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a sympathy card be?**
Two to five sentences is enough. A short, sincere note is better than a long one that feels forced.

**Should I send a card if I did not know the person well?**
Yes, if you know the family. A short card that says you are thinking of them is a kindness.

**Is it okay to send one late?**
Yes. A late card shows you kept thinking of them.

**Can I mention the cause of death?**
Only if the family has spoken about it openly. If you are unsure, leave it out.

**Will you illustrate the person who has died?**
No. We do not create illustrations of the deceased. You can write about them in your own words, and the art stays quiet and gentle.
