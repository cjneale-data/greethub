## 4. Positioning risks

1. Price is the only honest book claim today. Against Imagitime we lose on preview speed (about 2 min vs 5-15), reviews (3,640 at 4.8 vs zero), real art (none yet) and a printer-backed date (none). The $20 gap can close with one price cut by a backed competitor. Wonderbly is owned by Penguin Random House.
2. The differentiator "one likeness across cards and books" is unbuilt. Cards take no photos and Saved People holds names only. Do not claim it or "approve the look first" beyond what is live. "Better than other AI books" and "no training" stay off until legal confirms vendor ZDR.
3. AI backlash (Futurism, Wired) lands on the grandparent buyer. Privacy and "your words lead the story" help only if the cover label and the 90-day purge are visible and true.
4. Card pricing is out of line: $10.98 mailed against free to $7 at incumbents; the $8.99 postage-included scenario exists in the CFO model but the app still charges $10.98. Digital book $19.99 is above the free to $15 band; CFO v2's $14.99 is still high. Digital card $3.99 competes with free.
5. Photo cards: a November launch at flat prices will look high next to Shutterfly and Zazzle 40-50% October sales. Phase 1 without printed addressing is a visible gap (Minted, Zola, Zazzle free; Shutterfly $0.42). Zola owns weddings with free RSVP. Mail-for-me is an opportunity only if the printer can price near $1.49.
6. Delivery promise: the 7-day default beats every competitor without printer data. Hold delivery claims until sandbox runs.
7. Fit confidence stays at about 35-40%, conditional on real art and preview speed. Willingness to pay at $45-$65 has no survey behind it.
