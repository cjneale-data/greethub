# Photo cards and events: evals

Owner: Model quality and evals. Version 2026-10-15. Status: PROPOSED (wave 1).
Reads: docs/products/photo-cards-and-events-plan.md (4.3, 8), docs/cto/photo-events-architecture.md, app/lib/collage/ (place.ts, quality.ts as of 2026-10-15, uncommitted), pipeline/evals/ (golden-v0.json, score.ts).

Four things get evals: **photo ranking** ("Pick my best photos"), **auto-crop** (faces kept in frame), the **proof checker**, and **AI backgrounds**. For each: what "better" means, a golden set, how it is scored, and the launch bar. Every bar is stated as a point estimate *and* a lower confidence bound, because the sets are small and a 90% on 30 cases can mean 74%.

Spend to date for this work: **$0**. No model calls were made. Only the AI-background eval needs paid calls; its proposed cap is in section 5 and needs the owner's yes.

## 0. Ground rules for every set

1. **No real people, no customer photos, ever.** No family photos, no stock photos of real people, no public face datasets (they are real people too). Golden photos are made three ways, in this order of preference:
   - **P (procedural):** drawn by a script with `sharp` from fixed seeds: simple face proxies (skin-tone ovals with eye and mouth marks) on scenes, with blur, noise, exposure and clutter added on purpose. Ground truth (face boxes, which copy is blurred) is known by construction, so labelling is free and exact. Committed to the repo; regenerated by the script, so the repo holds the script, not the images.
   - **I (illustrated or 3D-style figures):** mannequin or clay-style figures, no likeness of anyone. Tests whether the crop still finds a subject when the "face" is not a photo face.
   - **S (synthetic photoreal adults):** AI-generated photoreal scenes of invented **adults only**, generated under a dollar cap, face boxes hand-labelled. **No synthetic children** until the owner and attorney say otherwise (B11 covers minors' photos; generating photoreal minors for test data is a question for them, not me). Holiday cards are full of children, so the gap matters: small faces are tested with adults at distance and with P images.
2. **Stored where:** P and I fixtures plus labels in `pipeline/evals/photo-events/` (wave 2). S images are generated files, not people, but are kept out of git (large, and to avoid anyone mistaking them for real people) and listed by hash in a manifest.
3. **Frozen per version.** Each set has an id (`pe-rank-v1`, `pe-crop-v1`, `pe-proof-v1`, `pe-bg-v1`). A change that touches a scored component reruns the whole set; results are compared to the last run on the *same* set, paired, never across set versions.
4. **Better, not different:** a change ships only if it beats the current version on the primary metric with a paired test (McNemar for pass/fail, sign test for scores) or ties it while fixing a named failure, and it does not regress any hard check.
5. **Uncertainty:** Wilson 95% intervals for rates and mean ± 1.96·SE for scores, as `pipeline/evals/score.ts` already does. Every report states n.

## 1. Photo ranking ("Pick my best photos")

**What it does (plan 4.3):** rank a customer's uploads by sharpness, faces and eyes open, then suggest a layout. No ranking code exists yet (checked 2026-10-15). The v1 the CTO's free-first approach implies is heuristic: Laplacian-variance sharpness, exposure clipping, resolution vs the slot (effective DPI), attention focus strength. **Eyes open and face count need a face model**, which v1 does not have; those rows below are scored but not gated until a model exists.

**Golden set `pe-rank-v1`: 40 photo sets, 4 to 12 photos each (about 300 photos).**

| Group | Sets | Built how | Truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near-duplicates: same scene, one sharp, others motion- or focus-blurred at 3 strengths | 12 | P, I | Sharp copy is best (by construction) |
| Exposure: correct vs under/over by 1.5 to 3 stops | 6 | P, S | Correct exposure is best |
| Too small for print: same scene at 4000 px and 600 px long edge | 6 | P | Large copy is best; small copy flagged |
| Eyes open vs closed, looking away | 6 | S | Two raters; only agreed labels kept |
| Mixed real-world-like sets (group, pet, snow, tree lights, dark indoor) | 10 | S, I | Two raters pick top 3; ties allowed |

**Metrics.**
- **Defect-pair accuracy** (primary): for every pair where one photo has a known defect (blur, exposure, too small), how often the clean one ranks higher. Built by construction, so no rater noise.
- **Defective-hero rate:** how often a photo with a known defect is ranked #1 in its set.
- **Top-3 recall of the raters' pick** on the mixed sets.
- **Rater agreement** (Cohen's kappa) on the mixed and eyes sets; if kappa < 0.4 the set is too ambiguous to gate on and is reported only.
- **Layout suggestion:** suggested template has as many photo slots as good photos (up to 4), and no slot gets a photo below 200 effective DPI when a larger one is unused.

**Launch bar (Phase 1, holiday cards).**

| Metric | Bar | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| Defect-pair accuracy, blur and too-small | ≥ 95%, lower bound ≥ 90% (n ≈ 150 pairs) | These are deterministic signals; anything less is a bug |
| Defect-pair accuracy, exposure | ≥ 90%, lower bound ≥ 80% | |
| Defective-hero rate | ≤ 5% of sets | The hero photo is the card |
| Top-3 recall of raters' pick | ≥ 70% (report only if kappa < 0.4) | Taste; ranking is a suggestion, the customer swaps freely |
| Eyes-open pairs | Report only until a face model exists | v1 cannot see eyes |

If the bar is missed, ship "Pick my best photos" as "Hide blurry and too-small photos" (a filter, not a ranking). That keeps the useful part and drops the claim.

## 2. Auto-crop (faces kept in frame)

**What it does today:** `analyzePhoto()` in `app/lib/collage/place.ts` takes one focus point from sharp's attention strategy on a 256 px copy (centre fallback when it returns nothing); `cropRect()` centres the slot-aspect crop on it and slides it inside the photo. One point, no face boxes. The eval measures whether that is good enough and is the gate for any face-model upgrade (architecture 4.2).

**Golden set `pe-crop-v1`: 80 photos x 3 slot shapes = 240 crop cases.** Slot shapes come from the real templates: full-bleed 5x7 portrait (incl. bleed), a 2-up landscape half, a square grid cell.

| Group | Photos | What it stresses |
|---|---|---|
| Single subject centred | 8 | Baseline; should be easy |
| Single subject near an edge (left/right/top third) | 12 | Centre crop fails here; attention must earn its keep |
| Group of 3 to 6 in a row, landscape photo into portrait slot | 12 | Hardest case: not every face can fit; which ones are lost |
| Small faces at distance (whole family in front of a house) | 8 | Holiday-card staple; faces ~3% of frame |
| **Distractors**: string lights, tree ornaments, red sweaters, bright window, saturated wrapping paper | 16 | Attention scores saturation and detail; a lit tree may beat the faces. Highest-risk group for December |
| Low-saturation scenes (snow, grey sky, black-and-white) | 8 | Skin-tone cue is weak |
| Pets with and without people | 8 | Pet faces should be kept too |
| Illustrated/3D figures | 8 | Subject that is not a photo face |

Ground truth per photo: every face box (by construction for P, hand-labelled for S and I, two labellers, boxes merged by IoU ≥ 0.5) and a "must keep" flag (faces in the main group; a stranger in the background is "may lose").

**Metrics, per crop case.**
- **Face kept:** ≥ 95% of the face box inside the **trim** box (not just the bleed, since bleed is cut off) and inside any frame/polaroid border.
- **Case pass:** every "must keep" face kept. When the slot physically cannot hold them all (group too wide for the aspect at zoom 1), the case is marked "infeasible" and passes if the kept set is contiguous and includes the centre-most faces.
- **Headroom:** top of the highest face box at least 3% of the crop height below the top edge (no foreheads cut on the trim line).
- **Text-slot collision:** no must-keep face under a text slot in templates where text overlays the photo.
- **Fallback rate:** share of photos where `focusSource` is `"center"`.
- **Baseline:** every run also scores the plain centre crop on the same cases. The engine must beat it on the paired cases (McNemar, p < 0.05), or the attention step is adding cost and no value.

**Launch bar (Phase 1).**

| Metric | Bar |
|---|---|
| Case pass, all groups | ≥ 90%, lower bound ≥ 85% (n = 240) |
| Case pass, distractor group | ≥ 85% (n = 48); if missed, see below |
| Case pass, small-faces group | ≥ 85% (n = 24) |
| Headroom violations | ≤ 5% of cases |
| Beats centre crop | Significant on the edge, group and distractor groups combined |
| Hard: a must-keep face fully lost (0% in frame) on a feasible case | ≤ 2% |

If the distractor group misses, the fix in order of cost: (1) compute attention on a desaturated copy, (2) restrict focus to skin-tone-weighted regions, (3) a free on-device face detector (eval item for wave 2; licence check needed before use). Whatever ships, the editor's pan/zoom is the safety net, and the proof shows the trim line.

## 3. Proof checker

**What it does (plan 4.3, architecture 4.4):** rules, no model, run on the text slots at "Review": names spelled consistently, weekday matches date, venue and time present, RSVP-by before the event, the right year on holiday cards, past dates. Shown as warnings with "Fix it" / "It's right" (docs/cx/07-photo-events-ux.md). Not built yet.

This one is deterministic, so the bar is high: a wrong date on 150 save the dates is the risk the plan names first.

**Golden set `pe-proof-v1`: 200 designs, about 60 clean and 140 with one seeded error each (plus 10 with two).** Each case is a JSON design (template id, text slot values, product, "made on" date) with the expected warnings. Names are invented. "Made on" dates are fixed so cases do not rot over time.

| Error class | Seeded cases | Examples (all verified with `date` on 2026-10-15) |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday does not match date | 25 | "Saturday, June 14, 2027" (a Monday; the plan's own example); "Sat 6/12/27" (correct, clean control); "Friday, December 24, 2026" (a Thursday) |
| Date does not exist | 6 | "February 29, 2027"; "June 31"; "13/06/2027" read US-style |
| Date in the past relative to "made on" | 8 | Save the date made 2026-11-01 for "June 12, 2026" |
| RSVP-by on or after the event | 15 | "Kindly reply by June 20" for a June 12 wedding; RSVP-by with no year crossing New Year |
| Missing venue or time (invitations) | 12 | Time blank; venue only "TBD" |
| Wrong or missing year on a holiday card | 15 | "Happy Holidays 2025" made Dec 2026; "Merry Christmas 2026" made Jan 2 2027 (warn); "Happy New Year 2027" made Dec 2026 (clean); year-in-review letter mentioning 2025 events (clean) |
| Name spelled two ways | 25 | "Katherine" front / "Kathryn" back; "The Riveras" vs "The Rivera Family" (clean); "Jon & Ana" vs "John & Ana"; "Rivera's" (possessive, clean); nicknames "Liz" vs "Elizabeth" (clean unless both in the same role) |
| Date formats the parser must read | 20 (mixed into the above) | "6.12.27", "June 12th", "12 June 2027", "Saturday the twelfth of June, two thousand twenty-seven" (formal wedding wording), weekday and date on separate lines |
| Clean designs | 60 | All of the above formats with correct values, so false positives are measured on hard cases, not easy ones |

**Metrics.** Per error class: **recall** (seeded errors caught) and **false-positive rate** (warnings on clean designs, and on correct fields of seeded designs). Also: every warning names the field it is about, so "Fix it" lands in the right place (scored as a separate pass/fail).

**Launch bar.** Bars apply to the classes in the phase that ships: Phase 1 = year, names, past date; Phase 2 adds weekday, nonexistent date, formats; Phase 3 adds RSVP-by, venue and time.

| Class | Recall | False positives |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday mismatch, nonexistent date, RSVP-by not before event, wrong holiday year | **100%** of seeded cases (these are arithmetic; any miss is a bug fixed before launch, and the case stays in the set) | ≤ 2% of clean designs |
| Past date, missing venue/time | 100% | ≤ 5% |
| Name spelled two ways | ≥ 90% | ≤ 5% (a nagging checker gets "It's right" clicked without reading) |
| Formal and unusual date formats | Parsed ≥ 90%; **an unparsed date gives a "check this date" notice, never silence** | |
| Warning names its field | 100% | |

Recall of 100% on n = 15 has a Wilson lower bound of only 80%, so these classes are also covered by unit tests in the card engineer's code (wave 2) that sweep every day of 2026 to 2028 for the weekday rule. The golden set checks the parsing; the sweep checks the arithmetic.

## 4. Auto-crop and DPI thresholds that disagree today (flag)

Three sources set the low-resolution rule differently:
- Architecture section 2: warn below 200 DPI, **block** below 150.
- `app/lib/collage/quality.ts`: warn below 200, "too_low" below **120**, and too_low is a strong warning, not a block ("still the customer's choice").
- CX doc: "A DPI block can't be dismissed".

The eval can measure either, but the product needs one rule. I recommend 200 warn / 150 block until a physical proof at each size shows where blur becomes visible; then set the block from that proof. **Owner/CTO decision**, and it needs the physical proofs only the owner can order.

## 5. AI backgrounds (border or backdrop)

**What it does (plan 4.1):** one render (~$0.06) of a border or backdrop in one of the 27 styles; the photos and text sit on top.

**Golden set `pe-bg-v1`: 27 styles x 3 occasions = 81 briefs**, occasions Christmas, Hanukkah, and neutral winter/New Year. Each brief names the template (so the photo windows and text slots are known), the occasion and the style id from docs/design/style-library.json. No people in any brief. 10 styles get a second seed to measure variance (91 renders total).

**Automated checks (free, scripted on each render):**
- **Resolution:** output covers the 5x7 page plus bleed at ≥ 200 effective DPI after any upscale (5.25 x 7.25 in at 300 DPI is 1575 x 2175 px).
- **Full bleed:** no white or cream frame on any edge (same check as the book pipeline's border measurement).
- **Text-slot contrast:** contrast between the template's text colour and the background under each text slot ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 for headline sizes); background busy-ness under text (local variance) below a threshold set from the first 20 renders.
- **Photo windows:** for borders, the art stays out of the photo windows (it will be covered, but a cut-off figure peeking past a photo edge looks like an error).

**Human rubric, 1 to 5 per render (two raters):** style fidelity (reads as the named style), occasion fit, harmony with a sample photo placed on top, print-readiness, and would-pay (yes/no). Hard checks, pass/fail: **no letters or words in the art** (templates set the text), **no people or faces** (they compete with the photos), no protected characters, logos or brand marks (docs/legal/07-ip-safety-design.md), **correct religious objects** (a Hanukkah menorah has 9 branches, 8 plus the shamash; a 7-branch menorah is a different object), no mixing of Christmas and Hanukkah symbols in one design.

**Launch bar (per style, so the style picker can hide weak ones):**

| Measure | Bar to offer the style for photo-card backgrounds |
|---|---|
| Hard checks | 3 of 3 occasions pass (any text-in-art, face, IP or wrong-menorah failure hides the style for that occasion) |
| Automated contrast and resolution | Pass on all 3 |
| Mean human score | ≥ 3.5 of 5 across dimensions, would-pay on ≥ 2 of 3 |
| Overall | ≥ 20 of 27 styles qualify, or AI backgrounds launch as "selected styles" with only those offered |

With 3 renders per style, a per-style result is a screen, not a measurement; the 10 second-seed styles give the variance estimate, and the report says so.

**Cost (proposed cap, needs the owner's yes):** 91 renders x ~$0.06 = ~$5.50, plus up to 25 re-renders for rubric calibration, **cap $8.00**. Results go into a per-style, per-model table (pass rate, mean score, cost per accepted background) for gh-head-of-design and gh-cfo. Default until the cap is approved: run the automated checks in mock mode (GREETHUB_MOCK_AI=1) so the scorer is tested end to end without spend.

Synthetic photoreal adults for sections 1 and 2 (group S, about 60 images): **cap $4.00**, same approval. Without them the ranking and crop sets run on P and I images only (about 70% of cases), and the report says which groups are missing.

## 6. Scoring and where results go

- Wave 2 extends `pipeline/evals/score.ts` with `--photo-rank`, `--crop`, `--proof` and `--bg` modes (or a sibling `score-photo-events.ts`), reading the golden JSON in `pipeline/evals/photo-events/` and printing each metric with n and its 95% interval, the bar, and PASS/FAIL. The crop and proof scorers call `app/lib/collage` functions directly: no model, no network, runnable in CI.
- Results are appended to `pipeline/evals/results/photo-events.jsonl` (set id, component version or git hash, metric, k, n, interval, spend). Spend is recorded even when $0.
- **Launch gate summary for Phase 1 (holiday photo cards):** auto-crop bars (section 2), proof checker bars for year, names and past date (section 3), ranking bars if "Pick my best photos" ships (section 1), and AI-background per-style bars for any style offered (section 5). Weekday, RSVP and venue bars gate Phases 2 and 3.

## 7. Not covered here

Illustrated family portraits (later phase; the book likeness evals apply, with the same consent rules). Greeting and letter writer quality (existing "Write it for me" evals). Address validation accuracy (shop engineer and fulfillment). Physical print fidelity (needs owner-ordered proofs).
